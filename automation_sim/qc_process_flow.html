Physical System Architecture

The system under study is a high-speed quality-control conveyor sorting cell. Cylindrical workpieces enter at the infeed, pass an optical trigger sensor ($PE_1$), are inspected for defects by vision/eddy-current sensor ($S_1$), and if defective, are ejected into a scrap bin by an orthogonal pneumatic actuator ($A_1$).

Control Mathematics & Spatial Discretization

In high-speed manufacturing, relying on fixed software timers creates drift and misalignment. Instead, the conveyor spatial window is discretized into $N$ discrete cells corresponding to the distance moved per scan cycle.

💡 Eliminating Quality Escapes using Lead Actuation Tuning

1. Why do Quality Escapes occur when $N_{\text{lead}} = 0$?
Pneumatic actuators suffer from physical stroke latency ($t_{\text{ext}} = 120\text{ ms}$). If controller logic fires the solenoid only when a part is already at the physical pusher position ($x = 0.60\text{ m}$), the belt velocity ($v = 0.20\text{ m/s}$) carries the part off the belt before the cylinder finishes extending. This results in defective parts spilling into the Good Parts Bin as Quality Escapes (Red).

2. How does sliding $N_{\text{lead}}$ bring Quality Escapes to Zero?
By moving the Lead Actuation Logic ($N_{\text{lead}}$) slider to match or auto-calculate optimal compensation ($N_{\text{lead}} = \frac{t_{\text{ext}}}{\Delta t} = 6\text{ cells}$), the PLC pre-triggers the solenoid 120 ms prior to part arrival. The cylinder reaches full extension exactly when the defective part aligns with the ejector, driving Defects Diverted to 100% and Quality Escapes to 0%!
